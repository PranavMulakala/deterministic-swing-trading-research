# Borrow Real's play-by-play, skip its casino

Your idea has a real basis, but only half of your thesis holds up. A single page with a ticker's live price, chart, news and chatter already exists on Stocktwits, TradingView, X (whose Smart Cashtags launched in April 2026), Yahoo Finance and, since September 29, 2026, Robinhood Social, which is open to every eligible customer of a broker with 28.6 million funded customers. What none of them has built is the engine that makes Real special: every play becomes its own conversation the moment it happens, an algorithm scores how big it was, your phone buzzes, and a crowd that showed up for that exact moment piles on. That is how Real pulls more than 250,000 people into the app at once during close games and gets nearly 30% of its users to comment or react. Demand for that experience in markets is everywhere: 60% of investors aged 18–34 use social media to make investing decisions, options trading is on pace for a record 18 billion contracts in 2026, and traders pay $29–$300 a month for Discord alert rooms whose posted results your own minute-by-minute price checks could not reproduce. The candid counterweight is a long graveyard of standalone social-investing apps, a model that almost never pays without a trading business attached, and Robinhood, X and Kalshi each shipping pieces of this right now. So yes, build it, but as a cheap experiment with a deadline aimed at one "league," earnings nights: a broker-neutral live room with an automatic feed of market plays, emoji reactions and a thread on every play, long/short/flat tags stamped with the live price when you post, and free predictions for points with no cash value, with no trading, no paid alert rooms and none of Real's card-pack economy. Servers are cheap; licensed market data is the real bill, about $3,500–$6,500 a month for a lean, legal setup at 1,000 users versus $17,000–$18,000 if every user got full exchange-grade stock and options feeds. Your first step costs close to nothing: hand-run a few earnings-night rooms in the back half of this Q3 season, the way Real's co-founder typed live TV events in by hand for four years before automating, with pass marks written down before the first room opens.

## Real turns every play into a thread, a push and a crowd

Real is the app listed in the stores as "Real - Sports," built by Real Vault, Inc. of Chicago. On October 18, 2026 it held **4.8 stars from 26,211 iOS ratings** and ranked #24 among free sports apps, with 4.3 stars and 500,000+ downloads on Google Play ([App Store](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/real-sports/id1514546162); [iTunes lookup](https://itunes.apple.com/lookup?id=1514546162&country=us); [Google Play](https://play.google.com/store/apps/details?id=vg.real&hl=en_US&gl=US)). Front Office Sports calls it a social box score, and the founders describe the concept as "inverting Twitter": the data point comes first and fans react to it, instead of people posting opinions that happen to mention a game ([Front Office Sports](https://frontofficesports.com/chernin-group-funding-round-real-app/)). It launched NBA-only in March 2021 with about **1,000 monthly users**, passed 100,000 roughly 14 months later, and with almost no paid marketing reached **1.3 million monthly and 700,000–900,000 daily users by April 2026** ([John Antonelli on LinkedIn, May 2022](https://www.linkedin.com/posts/johnmantonelli_startup-sports-tech-activity-6933466447604064256-4fSG); [Front Office Sports](https://frontofficesports.com/chernin-group-funding-round-real-app/)). This fall its partners describe **1.5 million+ monthly users, more than 250,000 people in the app at once during close games, over 70% of monthly users returning on big game days, and nearly 30% commenting or reacting** ([Flutter](https://flutter.com/news-media/blogs/how-fanduel-is-bringing-betting-into-the-social-conversation/)). Every one of those numbers is company- or partner-reported, and no independent app-intelligence estimate exists. The 30% is the number to remember: in most online communities almost everyone lurks, and Real gets close to a third of its users to say something ([TBPN interview](https://share.transistor.fm/s/e6c9bcc4/transcription)).

### Each play is a post with a score attached

Open a live game and you get a vertical play-by-play feed in which each play is a compact card carrying a **Real rating**, tags like "lead-taking" or "walk-off," a reaction counter and its own comment thread ([ScreensDesign](https://screensdesign.com/showcase/real-sports); [Real Rating System](https://docs.realapp.link/basics/real-rating-system.md)). The rating is the quiet genius of the product. An algorithm scores every play and performance on objective inputs, including how close and how late the game is, active streaks, shot distance, the shooter's efficiency and whether the play ties the game, takes the lead or wins it, on a scale that normally tops out at 10 but can break it on historic nights ([Real Rating System](https://docs.realapp.link/basics/real-rating-system.md); [TBPN](https://share.transistor.fm/s/e6c9bcc4/transcription)). That one number decides what gets surfaced, what triggers a push and what becomes a collectible, and it lets Real claim freedom from the media's bias toward stars. Around each play, basketball feeds add context a box score never shows: scoring runs, how long a team has gone without a field goal, free throws pending, possession and bonus, and a bar showing which team is covering the betting spread ([Changelog](https://docs.realapp.link/updates/changelog.md)).

Each play is also a social object you can act on. You can react with hundreds of custom emojis, with your favorites sitting as one-tap chips under every play and other people's reactions floating across your screen as they land; comment and reply with @mentions and GIFs; upvote or downvote; edit within 60 seconds with visible history; report; share a rendered image of the play to X or Instagram; save it to an album; jump to the player's whole game; tap out to video; and even collect the play as a card ([Changelog](https://docs.realapp.link/updates/changelog.md); [Comments](https://docs.realapp.link/interact/comments.md); [Play Cards](https://docs.realapp.link/collect/play-cards.md)). Crowd signals make the room feel alive: a "Pulse" number showing the peak count of people typing at once, impression counters on everything, a trending bar of whatever drew the most views in the past minute, and AI summaries that condense huge threads ([Changelog](https://docs.realapp.link/updates/changelog.md)). Around the feed sit a full-screen game chat, box-score tabs, a scrubbable momentum chart, friend and team groups, direct messages and a Discord server.

The other half of the loop is your phone buzzing. "Real Moments" pushes the tense final minutes of close games to fans of those teams, and sometimes to everyone, so a big share of users live the same moment together; team and player alerts fire on events like 12 straight points or a 40-yard play; Pro members can get an alert for every play type for any player; and iPhone users get Live Activities on the lock screen ([Notifications](https://docs.realapp.link/basics/notifications.md); [Real Pro](https://docs.realapp.link/basics/real-pro.md); [Changelog](https://docs.realapp.link/updates/changelog.md)). Real sends about **50 million pushes a day**, which on its April 2026 user count works out to roughly 38 per monthly user per day ([Front Office Sports](https://frontofficesports.com/chernin-group-funding-round-real-app/); my math). It never depended on licensed video either: in 2022 it had no video or images at all, and today plays link out to clips while the visuals on screen are data, such as shot charts, momentum graphs, NFL route replays and pitch tracking ([GrepBeat](https://grepbeat.com/2022/08/18/duke-senior-and-his-two-brothers-getting-real-about-sports-viewing-experience/); [Changelog](https://docs.realapp.link/updates/changelog.md)). ESPN's app, by contrast, is built around streaming tiers, studio shows and up to four streams at once, and a 2023 profile contrasted ESPN's home screen of opinions and headlines with Real's scores-and-stats-only home ([ESPN on the App Store](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/espn-live-sports-scores/id317469184); [BetCrunch](https://www.betcrunch.com/p/a-real-sports-app-thats-built-on)). Fox Sports' listing couldn't be retrieved, so I can't compare it directly.

One correction to your mental model shapes everything after it. Real feels insanely fast because TV is slow, not because the app runs in milliseconds. The founders say the feed often runs **a minute or two ahead of the broadcast**; fans testing it in stadiums report plays appearing about 5–10 seconds after they happen; and Real built a slowmode setting that delays the feed by 5–120 seconds precisely so it stops spoiling TV ([Front Office Sports](https://frontofficesports.com/chernin-group-funding-round-real-app/); [App Store reviews](https://itunes.apple.com/us/rss/customerreviews/page=1/id=1514546162/sortby=mostrecent/json); [Changelog](https://docs.realapp.link/updates/changelog.md)). Its edge is beating a delayed broadcast, and markets have no broadcast delay to beat.

### A free status game sits on top, and a paid card economy above that

Real wraps the feed in a free status game. Karma is a free points currency: you earn 10 for opening a live game and 10 each for your first comment and first reaction of the day, plus more for poll picks, and it unlocks a profile banner at 1,000, a flair emoji beside your name at 5,000, a Discord role at 10,000 and a "super upvote" at 50,000 ([Karma](https://docs.realapp.link/basics/karma.md)). Polls run before and during games: a pre-game pick against the spread where you can wager up to 100 karma, polls at quarter breaks, halftime and timeouts, and next-touchdown or next-goal polls, with the spread keeping blowouts interesting ([Polls](https://docs.realapp.link/interact/polls.md)). Badges, trophies and weekly, monthly and season leaderboards pay out in Real's currency, with a season's top three winning 1,600, 800 and 400 Rax ([Leaderboards](https://docs.realapp.link/interact/leaderboards.md)). The points double as spam control: in 2021 you needed 200 karma before you could comment at all, and new commenters were held to one top-level comment every five seconds until they reached 1,000 ([Changelog](https://docs.realapp.link/updates/changelog.md)).

Above that sits a paid economy. **Rax** is a virtual currency sold in $4.99–$49.99 bundles that can never be cashed out ([App Store](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/real-sports/id1514546162); [Rax](https://docs.realapp.link/basics/rax.md)). It buys serial-numbered player cards (200 Rax) and team cards (800 Rax), which pay their owners Rax after every game based on real performance, level up through seven rarity tiers, and display the card and serial number under the owner's comments on that player's plays ([Player Cards](https://docs.realapp.link/collect/player-cards.md)). Scarcity is engineered around game time: 300 game packs drop an hour before tip-off, and the price climbs from 800 to 1,250 Rax as they sell ([Card Packs](https://docs.realapp.link/collect/card-packs.md)). Since December 2025 there has been a peer-to-peer prediction market in Rax with early cash-out, alongside daily drafts and lineups and, as of October 17, 2026, a head-to-head fantasy mode called Arena that keeps 10% of each pot ([Changelog](https://docs.realapp.link/updates/changelog.md); [Arena](https://docs.realapp.link/interact/arena.md)). **Real Pro**, at $7.99 a month or $19.99 for three months, unlocks the web app, predictions, a free daily pack, per-play alerts, custom stat trackers and themes; an AI stats assistant called Real Bot launched the same day as Arena ([Real Pro](https://docs.realapp.link/basics/real-pro.md); [Real Bot](https://docs.realapp.link/interact/real-bot.md)). Since September 10, 2026, FanDuel has been Real's exclusive sportsbook and prediction-market partner, with odds inside game feeds, play views and box scores ([FanDuel](https://www.fanduel.com/about/news/real-sports-partner-with-fanduel)). The App Store now rates Real 18+ and lists simulated gambling and contests as frequent content, plus loot boxes ([App Store](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/real-sports/id1514546162)), which is why I call this layer the casino.

| Layer | What Real has | Copy it for markets? |
|---|---|---|
| Live feed | Play-by-play cards, Real rating, context tags, momentum chart, slowmode | Yes, this is the product (minus slowmode) |
| Per-play social | Emoji chips, threads, upvotes, Pulse counter, share images, game chat, groups | Yes, the core |
| Alerts | Real Moments, team and player alerts, Pro per-play alerts, Live Activities | Yes, with far fewer pushes |
| Status | Karma, badges, trophies, leaderboards, flair | Lightly, with free points only |
| Predictions | Spread polls with karma wagers, Rax prediction market, drafts, Arena | Free polls with no cash value only |
| Collectibles | Rax currency, player, team and play cards, packs, marketplace | Skip |
| Money | Pro at $7.99/month, Rax bundles at $4.99–$49.99, FanDuel odds | A Pro tier, yes; a sportsbook-style partner, no |

The pattern in that table is the most useful thing in this section. Real looks simple on day one, just scores and a feed, and grows complex over months. The clean feel you love lives in the first two rows; most of the feature count lives in layers bolted on later. An MVP can copy the feed and the threads and skip the rest.

### Fans love beating the broadcast and sweating it together

Reviews spell out why. In a tally of the 380 four- and five-star reviews among Real's 500 most recent US App Store reviews (April to October 2026), the top themes were speed (54 mentions of fast or instant updates), cards and Rax (46), comments and community (32), a clean, easy interface (32) and ESPN by name (27) ([App Store reviews](https://itunes.apple.com/us/rss/customerreviews/page=1/id=1514546162/sortby=mostrecent/json)). Fans describe getting plays before their TV does, reading the app's funny comments instead of watching the game, never opening an ESPN box score again, and bonding with strangers while their team melts down; one calls it ESPN and Twitter in one app (same source). The habit shows in the numbers: about three of every four monthly users came back weekly in both 2022 and 2024 ([John Antonelli on LinkedIn, May 2022](https://www.linkedin.com/posts/johnmantonelli_startup-sports-tech-activity-6933466447604064256-4fSG); [Louis Antonelli on LinkedIn](https://www.linkedin.com/in/louis-antonelli/)).

Speed alone isn't the moat, since FotMob, Yahoo Sports and theScore all advertise fast updates ([FotMob](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/fotmob-soccer-live-scores/id488575683); [Yahoo Sports](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/yahoo-sports-scores-and-news/id286058814); [theScore](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/thescore-sports-news-scores/id285692706)). The love comes from three things working together: seeing the play a beat ahead of the broadcast, a crowd reacting to that exact play within seconds, and a light status game that rewards simply showing up. Rivals attach chat to a whole game, team or league, when they have it at all; Real attaches it to each play. Incumbents are now copying pieces (Yahoo Sports added collectible "Milestones" and game threads, and Sleeper has live game chat), which tells you the mechanics are easy to copy and the moat is the crowd ([Yahoo Sports](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/yahoo-sports-scores-and-news/id286058814); [Sleeper](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/sleeper-fantasy-sports/id987367543)).

Who shows up matters for you. The founders say the audience skews 18–30 and that much of the recent growth came from fantasy players and bettors following their bets, and FanDuel cites Real's own research that about **75% of users who commented on player performances made betting-related comments** ([TBPN](https://share.transistor.fm/s/e6c9bcc4/transcription); [FanDuel](https://www.fanduel.com/about/news/real-sports-partner-with-fanduel)). Young, risk-seeking people who want company during the sweat are close cousins of retail options traders. The cold start is copyable too. Real's first thousand users came through John Antonelli's friends who run NBA meme and fan pages, such as NBA Central and Legion Hoops, whom he nudged to work Real's scoreboard into posts they were making anyway; he still encourages, and sometimes pays, creators to show Real's screens ([TBPN](https://share.transistor.fm/s/e6c9bcc4/transcription)). Real automated the top-stats-of-the-night graphics John once made by hand, claims billions of impressions from Real screenshots on X, Instagram and TikTok in 2025, and added leagues one at a time, timed to each season's start ([John Antonelli on LinkedIn, Jan 2026](https://www.linkedin.com/posts/johnmantonelli_what-a-year-for-real-sports-reached-1m-activity-7412933140261810176-uNxt); [Changelog](https://docs.realapp.link/updates/changelog.md)).

### A sportsbook deal dragged September's reviews to 2.9 stars

On September 10, 2026, the day the FanDuel deal was announced, Real got 71 App Store reviews; 68 were one or two stars, and 63 of those were about gambling or odds ([App Store reviews](https://itunes.apple.com/us/rss/customerreviews/page=1/id=1514546162/sortby=mostrecent/json)). September's reviews averaged **2.90 stars, against 4.93 in April**. Reviewers objected to betting pushed at a young audience, odds they say were switched on by default (the company calls them optional) and banner ads in an app that had been ad-free, and several cancelled Pro while quoting the founders' earlier promises to stay away from gambling money (same source; [FanDuel](https://www.fanduel.com/about/news/real-sports-partner-with-fanduel)). The whiplash is the lesson: in April the founders said their new Chernin Group money let them skip quick sportsbook and prediction-market sponsorships, and five months later they signed an exclusive one ([Front Office Sports](https://frontofficesports.com/chernin-group-funding-round-real-app/)).

The other complaints among the 107 one- and two-star reviews are the ones any live social app should plan for: greed and paywalls (23), lag, crashes and wrong plays under heavy traffic (17), and toxicity plus moderation that feels arbitrary (13), including racist comments and people wishing injuries on players. Even loyal users call the app confusing and overloaded (same source). Age gating is inconsistent as well: 18+ on iOS, "Everyone" on Google Play, and 13+ with parental consent in Real's own terms ([App Store](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/real-sports/id1514546162); [Google Play](https://play.google.com/store/apps/details?id=vg.real&hl=en_US&gl=US); [Terms of Use](https://docs.realapp.link/policies/terms-of-use)). Real's biggest weakness is trust, and a markets version faces the same trap in sharper form, because broker money, paid promotion and pump-and-dumps come with securities law attached.

### Five people, rented data and a meme-page network built it

Real was started by two brothers. Louis Antonelli, 34, studied computer science at Cornell and was the first engineer at Guilded, a Discord rival that Roblox bought for $90 million in 2021; John, 24, a 2023 Duke computer science graduate, grew the @bball Instagram account to roughly 900,000–1 million followers while in high school ([Front Office Sports](https://frontofficesports.com/chernin-group-funding-round-real-app/); [Wikipedia: Guilded](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Guilded); [Duke Magazine](https://dukemag.duke.edu/stories/entrepreneurial-vision)). The idea arrived in a 4 a.m. text in February 2020 about reacting to an NBA three with a Drake emoji ([FanDuel](https://www.fanduel.com/about/news/real-sports-partner-with-fanduel)). The precursor matters more. For about four years, Louis and his now-wife ran The Rose League, a fantasy game for *The Bachelor*, by **typing every event in by hand during live broadcasts**, and reached about 50,000 weekly users; Real is that format with an automated feed ([TBPN](https://share.transistor.fm/s/e6c9bcc4/transcription)). Real ran on the founders' savings through at least August 2022, raised a Chernin Group-led minority round of undisclosed size in April 2026 with NBA players and creators as angels, and at that point had **five people**: John on growth, Louis with two engineers, and their mother running finance ([GrepBeat](https://grepbeat.com/2022/08/18/duke-senior-and-his-two-brothers-getting-real-about-sports-viewing-experience/); [Front Office Sports](https://frontofficesports.com/chernin-group-funding-round-real-app/); [John Antonelli on LinkedIn, Apr 2026](https://www.linkedin.com/posts/johnmantonelli_in-february-2020-i-woke-up-to-a-text-from-activity-7449584221846773760-J85X)). With AI coding tools, that team went from shipping every three to four months to every two to three weeks ([TBPN](https://share.transistor.fm/s/e6c9bcc4/transcription)).

The technology is ordinary on purpose. Real buys data from commodity vendors such as Sportradar and Genius Sports, uses an official NHL feed, and scrapes the rest; John's view is that facts like a made three aren't owned by anyone, which matches the 1997 *NBA v. Motorola* ruling that game facts aren't copyrightable ([TBPN](https://share.transistor.fm/s/e6c9bcc4/transcription); [Wikipedia: NBA v. Motorola](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Basketball_Ass%27n_v._Motorola,_Inc.)). The stack is React and React Native, PostgreSQL and socket.io websockets in a single AWS region behind content-delivery networks ([Real web app bundle](https://realsports.io/ad70ad31/index.js); [Real job post](https://docs.realapp.link/jobs/full-stack-engineer-real)). Moderation runs cheapest-first: a lookup table catches about 80–85% of bad content, a fast classifier about 10% more, and a large language model handles what's left ([TBPN](https://share.transistor.fm/s/e6c9bcc4/transcription)). Design was done by the founding engineers until Real advertised for its first full-time designer ([Product Designer job post](https://docs.realapp.link/jobs/product-designer.md)). The takeaway cuts both ways. A tiny team can build this, so the technology is not the moat. But Real had two unfair advantages you don't have yet, an audience (John's meme-page network) and a founder who had already built real-time chat (Louis at Guilded), and its raw material was cheap, while yours is licensed per user.

## Your thesis is half right: the ticker page exists, the live room does not

Your instinct about fragmentation is right. Your belief that nobody puts a ticker's live price, chart, news and conversation on one screen is not, and the difference decides what you should build.

Startups are already in the lane. So the opening isn't being first to combine the pieces. Each incumbent fails on at least one of four things: the speed of its social layer, cleanliness, trust, or a crowd dense enough to feel like a game. The opening is making the combination feel live, clean and honest at the same time.

### Speed is table stakes in markets, and order lag isn't yours to fix

With no broadcast delay, a markets app can't win Real's way. Robinhood already shows you a live price. Human perception sets the bar: responses under a tenth of a second feel instantaneous ([Nielsen Norman Group](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/response-times-3-important-limits/)), and commodity tools deliver at that scale, with the open-source Centrifugo server pushing messages to a million connections at 200 milliseconds for 99% of deliveries and a single Phoenix server holding two million connections ([Centrifugo](https://centrifugal.dev/blog/2020/02/10/million-connections-with-centrifugo); [Phoenix](https://phoenixframework.org/blog/the-road-to-2-million-websocket-connections)). A well-built app should land around 100–300 milliseconds from data tick to screen, which is the research team's estimate rather than a measurement, and past that point extra speed is invisible. The speed you can own is how fast you detect and explain an event: "NVDA just broke its high of the day on triple its normal volume" appearing as a rated play with its own thread seconds after it happens, before anyone on X or in a Discord has typed it.

Your TradingView complaint is legitimate but mostly not yours to fix. On TradingView's own broker-review pages, tastytrade users describe 3–5 seconds on market orders and 10–15 seconds to adjust limit orders from the chart while trading same-day options; Trustpilot reviewers describe charts that won't load in volatile markets; and TradingView's status page logged trading incidents in January, March and June 2026 ([tastytrade reviews](https://www.tradingview.com/broker/tastytrade/reviews/); [Trustpilot](https://www.trustpilot.com/review/tradingview.com); [status page](https://status.tradingview.com/history)). TradingView's help center points to a structural cause: prices in its order panel come from your broker and are often delayed unless you buy the broker's real-time data, so the chart and the fill can disagree, while the free US stock feed is real-time but drawn only from Cboe's exchanges ([order panel help](https://my.tradingview.com/support/solutions/43000739323/); [market data help](https://in.tradingview.com/support/solutions/43000473924)). Orders travel through a bridge that each broker builds to its own standard ([TradingView brokerage integration](https://www.tradingview.com/brokerage-integration/)), and any app that routes orders through third parties inherits those hops, so "faster trading" is a weak wedge for a company that isn't a broker. Your real pain is better than that: Robinhood isn't a TradingView broker at all ([Brokers](https://www.tradingview.com/brokers/)), so your trading day spans three apps, with charts in TradingView, orders in Robinhood and calls in Discord. That is the honest version of your fragmentation thesis.

### Your audits found the biggest hole: nobody keeps honest score

You've already done research nobody in this market publishes. In your OWLS test, using a rule you wrote down before scoring (buy at the high of the minute after the alert, sell at the low of the minute after the exit post), **no analyst made money for a follower** between September 8 and October 8, 2026; the busiest same-day-options channel posted 106 alerts, 66 never got an exit post, and a follower risking $1,000 per alert would have lost about $45,600 ([OWLS results](/Users/pranavmulakala/personal/deterministic-swing-trading-research/output/OWLS_ALERTS_BACKTEST_RESULTS_20261008.md)). All 35 of Demon Alerts' screenshots were cropped strips of winning percentages; a follower buying in the minute after an alert paid on average **19% more than the posted price**, and up to 50% more; and at $450 per alert, every exit rule a follower could actually repeat lost between $3,128 and $5,652, while only selling at his final brag post, knowable only in hindsight, made money ([Demon review](/Users/pranavmulakala/personal/deterministic-swing-trading-research/output/DEMON_ALERTS_REVIEW_20261011.md)). EdwardAlerts VIP followers paid on average 15% above the posted price, and his own stated trim rule lost $1,215 at $300 per alert, against $3,593 for hindsight exits ([Edward VIP review](/Users/pranavmulakala/personal/deterministic-swing-trading-research/output/EDWARD_VIP_REVIEW_20261012.md)). A study of 15 paid Discord options-alert communities, reported by Bloomberg on October 7, 2026, found the same shape: recommended options jumped about 3% in the minute after an alert, subscribers generally lost money after costs even when they acted almost immediately, and the median contract had three days to expiry and about 30 times leverage ([Bloomberg](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-10-07/a-30-options-pop-in-mere-minutes-shows-power-of-finfluencers); [TokenPost summary](https://www.tokenpost.com/news/investing/29610)).

Robinhood's answer to fake screenshots is to post trades straight from its own ledger, which works only for Robinhood customers ([Fortune](https://fortune.com/2026/03/18/robinhood-rolls-out-long-awaited-social-feature-to-let-users-track-and-copy-other-traders/)). The calls your audience actually follows are made off-broker, in Discord, on X and in Telegram, where nobody stamps the price at the moment of the call and nobody computes what a real follower would have earned. That's the deepest gap in this market, and you've already built the fix for yourself: the scorer in your repo replays posted alerts against one-minute options prices with follower-realistic fills ([score.py](/Users/pranavmulakala/personal/deterministic-swing-trading-research/research/edward_alerts_v1/score.py)). Two cautions come with it. It runs on Alpaca's individual data plans, which are licensed for personal use and can't be shown to the public ([Alpaca data](https://alpaca.markets/data)), and publicly ranking other people's trade calls carries legal weight, covered in the risks below.

### Crypto and prediction markets prove the game, and show how it blows up

Outside stocks, "the market as a live game" is already a mass habit. pump.fun's coin pages put a live price, a live trade tape, a comment thread and a livestream on one page and have earned about **$1.16 billion** in cumulative revenue ([CryptoTicker](https://cryptoticker.io/en/pump-fun-revenue-buyback/)). Fomo, a clean mobile crypto app built around a live feed of real trades by people you follow plus profit leaderboards, raised $75 million at a **$550 million valuation in June 2026**, about 13 months after launching, and passed the power-user terminal Axiom in daily Solana volume in August, which is direct support for your "clean, simple UI" instinct ([TechTimes](https://www.techtimes.com/articles/318895/20260623/social-crypto-trading-app-fomo-raises-75m-sec-clears-non-custodial-wallets-operate.htm); [Crypto Briefing](https://cryptobriefing.com/fomo-surpasses-axiom-solana-trading-volume/)). Combined Kalshi and Polymarket volume went from **$2.0 billion a month in July 2025 to $53.0 billion in July 2026**, about 80% of it on sports ([Pew Research Center](https://www.pewresearch.org/short-reads/2026/09/23/prediction-markets-trading-volume-doubled-between-may-and-july-largely-driven-by-sports/)). The engine is identical every time: a scheduled event with a clock and a resolution, a price that moves on every play, and a crowd watching together.

The same corner shows what kills these products. A study of about 7 million pump.fun tokens found that **98.6% collapsed into pump-and-dumps**, and pump.fun suspended livestreams in November 2024 after people threatened self-harm and abused animals on camera to pump coins ([BeInCrypto](https://beincrypto.com/pump-fun-tokens-scams-solidus-labs-report/); [DL News](https://www.dlnews.com/articles/defi/pump-fun-revives-livestreams-after-chaos-cratering-volume/)). Making posts and people tradable spiked and crashed: friend.tech's token fell 98% before the team walked away, and Coinbase pulled the social feed from its Base App in February 2026, with its CEO conceding a month later that the social features hadn't worked ([DL News](https://www.dlnews.com/articles/defi/friend-tech-shuts-down-after-revenue-and-users-plummet/); [The Block](https://www.theblock.co/post/389191/base-app-removes-farcaster-powered-talk-feed-to-sharpen-focus-on-onchain-trading); [The Block](https://www.theblock.co/post/392080/coinbase-ceo-brian-armstrong-says-base-apps-socialfi-features-didnt-quite-work)). Scammers posted disguised phishing links in Polymarket comment sections and took more than $500,000 ([Phemex](https://phemex.com/news/article/hackers-exploit-polymarket-comments-for-500000-fraud-scheme-34686)). Two rules fall out of this: never pay anyone based on the price or trading volume of what they're talking about, and treat moderation and link-blocking as launch features, not add-ons.

## Demand is proven, but social investing rarely pays on its own

### Young, options-heavy traders already live on social media

The audience is big and in a mood to play. Robinhood alone had **28.6 million funded customers** in August 2026 ([Robinhood](https://investors.robinhood.com/news-releases/news-release-details/robinhood-markets-inc-reports-august-2026-operating-data)). FINRA's investor-education foundation found that **60% of investors aged 18–34 use social media to make investing decisions**, against 9% of those 55 and older, and that 61% of the young group has acted on a social-media personality's recommendation ([FINRA Foundation](https://www.finra.org/media-center/newsreleases/2026/finra-foundation-research-examines-characteristics-behaviors-outcomes)). Investors who use social media juggle **7.6 information sources versus 4.0** for those who don't, which is your fragmentation thesis measured, and 59% of them say entertainment and 59% say social activity motivate their investing, against 18% and 11% of non-users, which is the sports-fan parallel in one statistic ([FINRA Foundation brief](https://finrafoundation.org/sites/finrafoundation/files/2026-03/FINRA_Foundation_Research_Brief_Social_Media_Finfluencers.pdf)). And 43% of investors under 35 trade options ([FINRA Foundation, Dec 2025](https://www.finra.org/media-center/newsreleases/2025/new-finra-foundation-research-examines-shifting-investor-behaviors)). Options volume set a record of 15.21 billion contracts in 2025 and is on pace for more than 18 billion in 2026; same-day-expiry ("0DTE") options hit a record **66.2% of S&P 500 index option volume in July 2026**; and Cboe ties the June 2026 repeal of the pattern-day-trader rule to more trading from accounts under $25,000 ([FOW](https://www.fow.com/insights/us-options-volume-grows-a-quarter-in-2025-to-new-record); [Cboe](https://www.cboe.com/insights/posts/state-of-the-options-industry-options-market-continued-to-break-records-in-q-2-2026); [Cboe July 2026](https://ir.cboe.com/news/news-details/2026/Cboe-Global-Markets-Reports-Trading-Volume-for-July-2026/default.aspx)). Retail's share of that 0DTE flow is disputed, with Cboe saying individuals were 65% of customer 0DTE volume in May and OptionMetrics estimating 30–35%, but either way it is the most play-by-play-like thing retail traders do ([Investing.com](https://www.investing.com/news/company-news/cboe-q2-2026-slides-record-revenue-0dte-options-surge-to-65-of-spx-93CH-4828641); [FOW](https://www.fow.com/insights/optionmetrics-says-spx-0dte-retail-share-at-30-35)).

Three cautions temper this. New investors mostly aren't arriving: only 8% of investors started in the past two years, versus 21% in the 2021 survey, so you're competing for existing traders' attention rather than riding a new wave ([FINRA Foundation, Dec 2025](https://www.finra.org/media-center/newsreleases/2025/new-finra-foundation-research-examines-shifting-investor-behaviors)). Crypto is cooling: Coinbase's monthly transacting users fell to 7.6 million from 8.7 million a year earlier, and bitcoin sat about 36% below its October 2025 peak on October 18 ([Coinbase 10-Q](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/0001679788/000167978826000088/coin-20260630.htm); [Alpha Vantage data](https://www.alphavantage.co/documentation/)). And this audience gets hurt the most: investors who use social media were targeted by fraud at 13% versus 3% for others and lost money 68% of the time when targeted versus 29%, and Americans reported **$2.1 billion** lost in 2025 to scams that started on social media, more than half of it to investment scams ([FINRA Foundation brief](https://finrafoundation.org/sites/finrafoundation/files/2026-03/FINRA_Foundation_Research_Brief_Social_Media_Finfluencers.pdf); [FTC](https://www.ftc.gov/news-events/data-visualizations/data-spotlight/2026/04/reported-losses-scams-social-media-eight-times-higher-2020)). Trust and safety is a core cost of serving these people, not an extra.

The survivors fit two patterns. Social works as a feature bolted onto a trading business: eToro turns copying into commission-bearing trades, and Robinhood's Social is free because Robinhood earns on the trades. Even so, eToro spent $500 million of its $1.2 billion revenue on marketing in 2021, and its creator side is a steep power law, with about 3,300 "Popular Investors" (roughly 0.1% of funded accounts) and only 12 of them carrying more than $10 million in copied money ([OMR](https://omr.com/en/daily/omr-podcasat-etoro-yoni-assia); [Sacra](https://sacra.com/chat/h/d9dcbcc5-e54d-4699-b54a-c12461eb1977/)). Or social works as a modest niche: Blossom, a verified-portfolio social network, reached 750,000+ members and more than $6.5 million in annualized revenue in Q2 2026, roughly $8–9 per member per year, with brand partnerships making up about two-thirds of its revenue as of 2024 ([BetaKit, 2026](https://betakit.com/blossom-social-raises-more-than-2-million-through-frontfundr-campaign); [BetaKit, 2024](https://betakit.com/blossom-closes-2-1-million-to-grow-its-investing-social-network-in-the-us/)). Public's social feed produced none of Public's revenue ([Contrary Research](https://research.contrary.com/company/public)), and the exits in this category have been quiet tuck-ins into companies that already had distribution.

Without becoming a broker, three money paths are open, in roughly the order Real used them. First, a Pro tier around $8–$10 a month that bundles real-time quotes (licensed only for the people who pay), per-play alerts and a multi-room web view. Second, flat-fee broker referrals: affiliate networks list about $20–$70 per funded account for Webull and about $40 for moomoo, while Robinhood spent about $222 in marketing per net new funded customer in 2025 ($399 million across 1.8 million), which suggests room for better custom deals with a high-intent audience ([CommissionDex](https://commissiondex.com/programs/webull/); [FlexOffers](https://www.flexoffers.com/affiliate-programs/moomoo-affiliate-program/); [Robinhood 10-K](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1783879/000178387926000023/hood-20251231.htm); [Robinhood Q4 2025](https://investors.robinhood.com/news-releases/news-release-details/robinhood-reports-fourth-quarter-and-full-year-2025-results)). Third, later, licensing the live play-by-play as media, the way Kalshi's odds now run on CNBC ([The Block](https://www.theblock.co/post/381415/kalshi-exclusive-cnbc-deal-prediction-markets-surge-mainstream-media)). Be clear-eyed about the math: 10,000 referred accounts a year at $40 is $400,000, a supplement rather than a business, and a $10 tier converting 5% of 100,000 monthly users is $50,000 a month. A real company here needs six figures of monthly users. Real crossed 100,000 about 14 months after launch, in sports, with a co-founder's million-follower account and a ring of friendly meme pages behind it.

## Start with earnings night in a lean, broker-neutral room

### The wedge is a market moment with a clock and a final score

Real won by packing one league's fans into per-play threads on game nights instead of spreading them across every topic. Markets have game nights too: the 9:30 open, the 8:30 a.m. CPI print, the Fed's afternoon decisions, the last hour of 0DTE trading, and above all **earnings nights**. Earnings are scheduled weeks ahead, last an hour or two, are dramatic (the numbers hit, the stock gaps, the call begins) and end in a verdict, beat or miss. They're already watched live in options Discords. That's your first league. Start with large, liquid companies, which draw the biggest crowds and are far harder to pump than microcaps, so you shrink your manipulation risk from the first night.

| Real, in sports | Your version, in markets |
|---|---|
| A game | A scheduled market moment: an earnings release, CPI, a Fed decision, the open, the 0DTE close |
| A play | A price break (a new high of the day), a volume spike, a trading halt, an SEC filing, a headline, the EPS number, an unusual options print |
| The Real rating | A "move rating" that scores how unusual a play is: size versus normal, volume versus average, time of day, whether a catalyst exists |
| A Real Moments push | A push when a rated play crosses a threshold during a game you follow |
| A spread poll with karma | "Beat or miss?" and "Green or red at the close?" for free points |
| Card-holder flair | A long, short or flat tag stamped with the live price when you post, verified through your broker later |
| Leaderboards | Prediction accuracy, never profit and loss |
| Slowmode | Not needed, because everyone sees the same price at once |
| Rax, packs and cards | Leave out |

Broker neutrality is what makes the room yours. Robinhood Social works only for Robinhood customers, Kalshi's chat covers only Kalshi's contracts, and X's cashtags live inside a ranked general feed. A room that welcomes someone trading on Robinhood, Webull, Schwab or tastytrade alike, and stamps everyone's stance with the same live price, sits in the one lane those incumbents structurally won't take. Be honest with yourself that even this lane is contested. Robinhood promised a live earnings experience, where customers watch calls and trade in real time, in time for this Q3 season, though its support page says it is rolling out later; Google Finance has run live earnings calls with synced transcripts and AI summaries since October 30, 2025; and moomoo livestreams earnings calls with live comments ([Robinhood newsroom](https://robinhood.com/newsroom/hood-summit-2026/); [Robinhood support](https://robinhood.com/us/en/support/articles/hood-summit-2026-product-announcements/); [PPC Land](https://ppc.land/google-finance-ai-tools-reach-100-countries-with-local-language-support/); [Tencent RTC](https://trtc.io/blog/details/moomoo-tencent-rtc-chat-live-solution)). You win the night only on what those lack: a thread on every play, a dense crowd, broker neutrality and an honest scoreboard. Crypto belongs in the plan as weekend rooms later, because its data is the cheapest and it trades around the clock, but with crypto cooling and carrying the worst scam history of any asset class, it is a poor first league.

### The MVP is a web room where every play gets its own thread

Build for the web first. It's the fastest thing to ship, it runs on a desktop next to your broker during market hours, and it lets you learn before you take on App Store review. A mobile app should follow once the rooms prove themselves, because push notifications are where retention lives: in one vendor's data, users who got at least one push in their first 90 days were three times more likely to stick around ([GetStream, citing Airship](https://getstream.io/blog/app-retention-guide/)).

The room itself is simple. A schedule lists upcoming games from earnings and economic calendars, and each game gets one room. The header shows a licensed live price and a chart drawn with TradingView's Lightweight Charts library, which is free for commercial use as long as you show a TradingView attribution link ([Lightweight Charts](https://github.com/tradingview/lightweight-charts)). Below it runs an automatic play tape. Many of the best triggers are free and fast: the SEC's EDGAR system typically shows a new filing in its API in under a second, Nasdaq's trading-halt feed refreshes every minute, and the Fed publishes its meeting dates more than a year ahead ([SEC EDGAR APIs](https://www.sec.gov/search-filings/edgar-application-programming-interfaces); [Nasdaq halts feed](https://www.nasdaqtrader.com/rss.aspx?feed=tradehalts); [Federal Reserve](https://www.federalreserve.gov/monetarypolicy/fomccalendars.htm)). Headlines and live earnings-call transcripts come from paid vendors whose prices you'll have to request, and unusual options flow is available from Unusual Whales starting around $750 a month ([Quartr](https://quartr.com/api); [Unusual Whales](https://unusualwhales.com/pricing/enterprise)). Each play gets emoji chips, short comments (Kalshi caps live chat at 300 characters, which is worth copying), a reply thread, a live "people here" counter and a share button that renders a watermarked box-score image for creators to post. Each user carries a long, short or flat tag stamped with the live price when they post; it starts self-reported and can later be verified through a read-only brokerage link from an aggregator like SnapTrade at $1–$2 per connected user per month on top of a $100 monthly base ([SnapTrade](https://snaptrade.com/pricing)). Free "beat or miss?" predictions for points, with a leaderboard for accuracy, give people a reason to arrive before the numbers hit.

Moderation ships on day one, built the way Real built it: cheap keyword filters first, a fast classifier next and an AI model only for what's left, with new accounts slowed down and barred from posting links, and report, block and mute working from the first room ([TBPN](https://share.transistor.fm/s/e6c9bcc4/transcription)). The left-out list matters as much as the feature list: no trade buttons, no paid alert rooms or creator subscriptions, no broker- or company-paid promotion, no AI "ideas for you" built from someone's holdings, no real-money predictions or prizes, no tokens, packs or in-app currency, and no livestream payouts. Keep AI to factual jobs like labeling plays, with confidence labels and fast corrections; Bloomberg corrected at least three dozen of its AI news summaries in about three months of 2025, and at play-by-play speed your mistakes will be visible to everyone ([TalkingBizNews](https://talkingbiznews.com/media-news/bloomberg-has-a-rocky-start-with-ai-summaries/)).

Nothing in the engineering is exotic. Real runs on React, Postgres and socket.io; open-source servers such as Centrifugo or Phoenix handle huge audiences on a few cheap machines; and managed services like Ably and Pusher have free tiers covering the first 100–200 simultaneous connections ([Ably](https://ably.com/pricing); [Pusher](https://pusher.com/channels/pricing/)). Two tricks from Discord's engineering posts matter once rooms get hot: send reaction counts instead of one message per reaction, and don't push updates to rooms nobody has open, which cut Discord's fan-out work by about 90% in its largest server ([Discord](https://discord.com/blog/maxjourney-pushing-discords-limits-with-a-million-plus-online-users-in-a-single-server)). With AI coding tools, a technical solo founder can build this; Real's three engineers ship every two to three weeks the same way.

### Data, not servers, sets the bill

Here's the part most first-time builders miss: stock and options prices are licensed by the exchanges, per person who sees them, and the cheap developer plans can't legally power a public app. Massive's $29 plan (Massive is the renamed Polygon.io) and the individual tiers from Tiingo and ThetaData are for personal use only ([FISD](https://fisd.net/polygon-io-is-now-massive); [Massive pricing](https://massive.com/pricing); [Tiingo](https://www.tiingo.com/about/pricing); [ThetaData](https://www.thetadata.net/pricing)). You have four kinds of stock price to choose from. Fifteen-minute delayed data is close to free and carries no exchange fees at all under the new consolidated-tape plan that starts April 1, 2027, though you still need a license ([CT Plan](https://www.consolidatedtape.com/fees)). Modeled real-time prices are the cheap live option: Massive's $2,499-a-month Stocks Business plan streams a computed "fair market value" with no exchange fees or approvals, discounted 25% or more in the first year for qualifying startups, but it is an estimate rather than an exchange quote, so it can differ from what you see in Robinhood and must be labeled ([Massive business](https://massive.com/business); [Massive FMV docs](https://massive.com/docs/websocket/stocks/fair-market-value.md)). Single-venue exchange prices come from Cboe One at $0.25 per non-professional user, counted against a $5,000 monthly distribution fee, plus $1,000 for consolidation, or a flat $15,000 a month for informational, non-trading websites and apps; Cboe says its quotes sit within 1% of the national best price 97.95% of the time ([Cboe One](https://www.cboe.com/market_data_services/us/equities/cboe_one/)). Full consolidated prices from every exchange, known as the SIP, cost about $3 per user per month today plus about $6,500 a month in fixed fees, with per-user fees sliding from $0.90 to $0.25 per tape under the new plan ([CTA](https://www.ctaplan.com/publicdocs/ctaplan/Schedule_of_Market_Data_Charges.pdf); [UTP](https://www.utpplan.com/DOC/Datapolicies.pdf); [CT Plan](https://www.consolidatedtape.com/fees)). Real-time options quotes from the official OPRA feed add $1.25 per user plus $1,500 a month, or $650 if users tap to load a chain instead of streaming it ([OPRA](https://cdn.opraplan.com/documents/OPRA_Fee_Schedule.pdf)). Crypto is cheapest: Coinbase's public price feed needs no key, and licensed aggregators run $79–$129 a month ([Coinbase](https://docs.cdp.coinbase.com/exchange/websocket-feed/overview); [CoinMarketCap](https://coinmarketcap.com/api/pricing/); [CoinGecko](https://www.coingecko.com/en/api/pricing)).

| Monthly users | Lean, legal stack | Full real-time stocks and options for everyone |
|---|---|---|
| 1,000 | About $3,500–$6,500/month | About $17,000–$18,000/month |
| 10,000 | About $3,500–$8,500/month | About $50,000–$57,000/month |
| 100,000 | About $5,000–$38,000/month | About $345,000–$465,000/month |

These are the research team's estimates built from the published fee schedules above. The lean stack combines modeled real-time prices, delayed consolidated data, delayed or tap-to-load options, a crypto aggregator, free event feeds and $25–$400 a month of servers and chat, and its low end assumes you host the real-time servers yourself; the usage assumptions behind the server costs are unsourced and need a prototype to confirm. The realistic middle is a hybrid, where everyone gets modeled or delayed prices and only paying members get full exchange real-time. At 100,000 monthly users with 5,000 subscribers, real-time stocks and options for the payers add about $27,000 a month, which a $10 tier at 5% conversion ($50,000) covers. Two practical notes: if you license consolidated data directly rather than through a vendor, the new plan's agreements must be signed by March 1, 2027 ([consolidatedtape.com](https://www.consolidatedtape.com/)), and nobody has priced the legal review you'll need before launch, so budget for it separately.

The legal lines are clearer than they look, and this is research, not legal advice. The SEC's guide to broker registration flags pay that depends on the size or outcome of a trade, taking part in soliciting or executing trades, and handling customers' money ([SEC guide](https://www.sec.gov/reportspubs/investor-publications/divisionsmarketregbdguidehtm.html)), and FINRA's Rule 2040 bars brokers from paying unregistered people who should be registered ([FINRA Rule 2040](https://www.finra.org/rules-guidance/rulebooks/finra-rules/2040)). The workable pattern is a flat fee per referred lead or funded account, never per trade; when FINRA fined M1 Finance $850,000 over a flat-fee program with about 1,700 influencers, it cited the posts and the lack of supervision, not the payment structure ([FINRA](https://www.finra.org/media-center/newsreleases/2024/finra-fines-m1-finance-850000-violations-regarding-use-social-media)). The publisher exclusion the Supreme Court applied in *Lowe v. SEC* protects commentary that is genuine, impersonal and published on a regular schedule rather than timed to market moves ([Lowe v. SEC](https://www.law.cornell.edu/supremecourt/text/472/181)), which puts real-time paid alert rooms, callers who trade around their own calls and AI advice tailored to someone's portfolio at its weakest end. Section 230 shields platforms from liability for users' posts but not from federal criminal law or from content the platform develops itself, and the Third Circuit has treated algorithmic recommendations as the platform's own speech, so be careful with pushes that hype a hot ticker ([47 U.S.C. 230](https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/47/230); [Anderson v. TikTok](https://www2.ca3.uscourts.gov/opinarch/223061p.pdf)). The fact pattern prosecutors pursue is consistent: promoters in Twitter and Discord rooms say they're buying or holding and then sell into their followers, as in the roughly $100 million Atlas Trading case the Fifth Circuit revived in October 2025 and the 2023 case against a Discord trading-room moderator ([SEC](https://www.sec.gov/newsroom/press-releases/2022-221); [Justia](https://law.justia.com/cases/federal/appellate-courts/ca5/24-20143/24-20143-2025-10-02.html); [Investment Executive](https://www.investmentexecutive.com/news/regulation/sec-charges-trading-chatroom-moderator/)). Anyone paid by a company to talk up its stock must disclose who paid and how much, which is what cost Kim Kardashian $1.26 million over a crypto promotion, and the FTC's 2024 rule bans fake reviews and bought followers, so don't seed fake users to make rooms look busy ([15 U.S.C. 77q](https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/15/77q); [SEC](https://www.sec.gov/news/press-release/2022-183); [FTC](https://www.ftc.gov/news-events/news/press-releases/2024/08/federal-trade-commission-announces-final-rule-banning-fake-reviews-testimonials)). Free predictions for points with no cash value stay clear of gambling law because nothing is paid in and nothing of value is won, while a cash-settled "will NVDA close above $200?" contract on a single stock is a security-based swap, and Apple doesn't allow binary-options apps at all ([15 U.S.C. 78c](https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/15/78c); [Apple guidelines](https://developer.apple.com/app-store/review/guidelines/)). Real-money event contracts are being fought over in court and in rulemaking right now, with circuit courts split and a new CFTC proposal from October 9, 2026, so in-room odds from a partner like Kalshi are a later, lawyer-led decision ([CFTC](https://www.cftc.gov/PressRoom/PressReleases/9310-26); [Yogonet](https://www.yogonet.com/international/news/2026/10/08/126758-gaming-regulators-urge-us-supreme-court-to-hear-kalshi-case-decision-could-come-by-june-2027)).

That leaves a clean v1 lane: licensed data, news, user commentary, stance tags and free points, under terms that say information and entertainment only, with an 18+ gate, finance-specific rules (disclose positions and any payment, no paid promotion, extra friction on OTC and microcap tickers, no guaranteed-return claims), every post and edit stored with exact timestamps, and a written rule that you and anyone you hire won't trade the tickers you're featuring. Both app stores require filtering plus report and block tools for any app with user posts, platforms built on user content have needed a 48-hour takedown process for nonconsensual intimate images since May 19, 2026, and designating a copyright agent is a condition of the safe harbor that protects you when users upload infringing material ([Apple guidelines](https://developer.apple.com/app-store/review/guidelines/); [Google Play](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/9876937); [47 U.S.C. 223a](https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/47/223a); [17 U.S.C. 512](https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/17/512)). Gamification carries its own scrutiny: Robinhood dropped its confetti animation in 2021 as regulators looked at gamified investing ([NBC Boston/CNBC](https://www.nbcboston.com/news/business/money-report/robinhood-gets-rid-of-confetti-feature-amid-scrutiny-over-gamification-of-investing/2343214/?amp=1)). A startup lawyer should review all of this before launch.

One more risk is you, and I say it as your co-founder. You're a few months into markets, building for the group that FINRA finds is most often targeted and most often hurt by fraud, and Real's founders brought an audience and a chat engineer that you'll need to borrow. Your assets are real, though. You are the user. You write your test rules down before you look at results. And you hold receipts on paid alert groups that nobody else has published. Before writing much code, find one or two creators who would welcome an honest scoreboard; that's the John Antonelli role, and it matters more than any feature.

## Conclusion

Real's lesson for markets isn't speed or a clean interface; FotMob and Robinhood have both. It's the editorial decision about what counts as a play: an objective score that chops a stream of data into discrete, shareable, pushable moments, each with its own crowd. Basketball arrives pre-chopped into plays and markets don't, so the move rating that decides which price break, filing or options print deserves a thread is the piece you'd actually be inventing, and whoever defines the play owns the product. The second shift is that the most valuable number in trader social isn't the price; it's the price at the instant someone made a call compared with what a follower could really get. Robinhood can stamp that only for its own customers, Discord callers would rather nobody stamped it, and you've already shown you can compute it. If the rooms hold a crowd, that honest scoreboard, built carefully alongside a lawyer, is the moat a broker feed or a ranked timeline can't easily copy.

The economics argue for sequencing over ambition. A hand-run test costs a few weeks of evenings, while a full build commits you to $3,500 or more a month in data, plus legal work, before you know whether anyone shows up. Robinhood's October 27 earnings call and its own live earnings product will show within weeks how crowded the broker-native version of this lane is getting. Run the rooms, keep the score you wrote down in advance, and let the crowd, not the idea, decide whether this becomes a company.
