## 1. The one-sentence story
AI chips need a special stacked memory (HBM) that eats factory space. That made all memory scarce and sent its price up, so Micron, one of only three big memory makers, is earning record profits.

## 2. Proof in the numbers

| Q3 FY25 | Mar–May 2025 | $9.30B | +37% | 37.7% |

**Notes on the table:**
- **Gross margins are GAAP.** Micron's headline "non-GAAP" margins are slightly higher (87.0% in Q4).
- **Q4 FY26 had 14 weeks, not 13.** Micron's year ends on the Thursday nearest Aug 31, so the +379% is a little flattered. Per week it was about +345%.
- **The full year:** $133.2B of sales, up from $37.4B.

**The checklist questions:**
- **Is growth speeding up?** Yes, every quarter through August.
  - The guidance implies the first small slowdown in the growth rate (about +351%).
  - Sales in dollars still rise.
- **Are margins rising?** Yes, from 38% to 87%. That leaves little room to go higher. Management called about 86% the low point for this fiscal year.
- **Did it guide above Wall Street?** Yes: $61.5B, against about $57.0B expected (LSEG).
- **Is there a backlog or multi-year contracts?** Yes:
  - about **$150B of signed future revenue**, up from about $100B a quarter earlier;
  - 26 multi-year "take-or-pay" deals (customers pay even if they don't take the memory);
  - $32B committed by customers, mostly as cash deposits;
  - over 75% of FY27 shipments already allocated or under contract.
  - **The catch:** many contracts set a price **ceiling** as well as a floor.

**Where the growth came from** (Q4 sales by business unit, from the 8-K):

|---|---|---|---|

- **Micron's own description of Q4** (prepared remarks):
  - DRAM prices rose by a high-teens % in one quarter, and NAND (flash) prices by about 30%.
  - DRAM volume grew only by a mid-single-digit %.

## 3. Lasting or one-off?
**Why it could last (through 2027, at least):**
- **The contracts above:** most of FY27 is spoken for, backed by cash deposits.
- **Management's view:** supply will be much tighter in 2027 and 2028 than in 2026, and it can't see when supply and demand will balance.
- **HBM soaks up capacity:** it will use about 22% of the industry's DRAM wafers for about 9% of its output (TrendForce, end-2026 estimate).
- **New factories take years:** Micron's new ones start output in mid-2027 and late 2028.
- **TrendForce's 2027 outlook:** DRAM even tighter, and average HBM prices up 121%.

**Why it might not:**
- **It's mostly price, and the price gains are slowing.** Conventional DRAM contract prices (TrendForce):
  - Q2 2026: about +60%;
  - Q3: +13–18% (forecast);
  - Q4: +10–15% (forecast).
- **Everyone is building more factories:**
  - Micron's capex goes from $27.4B (FY26) to over $50B (FY27).
  - Samsung is enlarging its P5 fab.
  - SK hynix reportedly aims to double its DRAM wafer capacity by 2030.
  - China's CXMT reached 9.5% of DRAM sales, up from 7.6% a quarter earlier.
  - Most of the new output arrives in late 2027 and 2028.
- **Customers are pushing back on cost:**
  - memory is projected at about 68% of the big cloud companies' 2027 capex (TrendForce);
  - chip designers are weighing 8-layer HBM instead of 12-layer to save money.
- **TrendForce sees NAND (flash) loosening in late 2027,** which conflicts with Micron's view.
- **History:** every past memory boom ended with prices falling.
  - **2018:** the stock peaked in May while Micron was still reporting records, then fell 54%. FY19 earnings per share fell 47%.
  - **2022:** the stock peaked in January, again during record quarters, then fell 50%. FY23 revenue fell 49%, with a $5.8B loss.

**Honest read:**
- **LITE's growth** came from a new product that customers kept needing more of.
- **Micron's growth** comes from a shortage. Shortages last until they don't, and this one has a visible end window: new factories in late 2027–2028.
- **In past cycles, MU's price turned months before its earnings did.**
- **The checklist can't settle this step.** It's the one to keep checking.
