# The LITE playbook: a business-conviction checklist (October 7, 2026)

**Why this exists:** the owner's best result (about $40k on LITE in 2026) came from **understanding a business change before the crowd**, sized sensibly, with a simple exit rule.

Across 24 tests, chart-timing rules added little. **What you own and how much you put in** mattered most. This checklist turns the LITE experience into steps you can repeat for any company. It's research, not advice: decisions stay with the owner.

## The checklist (fill in one per company, before buying)
1. **The one-sentence story.** What changed in the business, and why now? (New product, new customer type, a supply shortage, a regulation…) If you can't say it in one sentence, you don't have conviction yet.
2. **Proof in the numbers (last 4–6 quarters).**
   - Is revenue growth **speeding up**?
   - Are gross margins rising?
   - Did the company guide **above** Wall Street's estimates?
   - Are there backlogs or multi-year contracts?
3. **Lasting or one-off?** Who is buying, and will they keep buying for years (AI data-center build-outs), or is it a temporary bump?
   - Test #20 showed most "sales speed-ups" are one-offs: vaccines, reopenings, metal prices.
   - This judgment is the edge.
4. **What could break it.**
   - competitors adding supply;
   - one big customer leaving;
   - the industry cycle turning;
   - interest rates;
   - the stock having already priced it all in.
5. **Price check.**
   - **How far it has already run.**
   - **Valuation vs its own history:** a low P/E can be a trap at a cyclical earnings peak.
   - **Its worst drops in the last year,** so you know what holding it feels like.
6. **Dates that matter:** its earnings, its biggest customers' and competitors' earnings, and product events.
7. **The position plan (written before buying).**
   - **Size:** the most you'll put in, as a % of your money, so a −40% drop is survivable.
   - **Entry:** all at once, in 2–3 pieces, or on Plan B dip days.
   - **Exits, written as prices:** a profit-taking level, a "review" level, and an "I'm wrong" level on a closing basis (for MU you wrote +25% / −10% review / −20% exit).
   - **What news would make you sell even if the price is fine:** the story breaking.
8. **Track it forward.** Add it to the forward watch (`research/forward_watch_v1/ideas.json`) so its dollars show up in the 4:45 PM email next to SPY.

## Rules of thumb from our tests
- **Concentration is where both the big wins and the big losses came from** (#17: +$299k profit, but −$439k from its high in 11 weeks). Size each idea so one bad one can't sink you.
- **A dip-buying rule only works on a stock that keeps rising.** Test #15 lost on 100 other volatile stocks. So the business research comes **first**, and Plan B is only for entries and exits.
- **Review after earnings, not after every red day.** On a quiet day, most day-to-day moves are noise.
