## 1. The claim, checked against the screen
- **What he says:** the account went "from a starting balance of $2,000 to over $65,000," and was up 44% on day 30 [0:00:56]. He says he's known for turning less than $600 into "more than $20 million of fully verified and independently audited trading profits" [0:00:04].
- **On screen, his trade journal (Tradervue) for the challenge:**
  - **total gain $63,731.64** over **68 trades**
  - **52 winners (76.5%)** and 16 losers
  - largest gain about **$11,524**, largest loss about **−$5,288**
  - **profit factor about 4.06**
- **Broker screen on Aug 5:** overall year-to-date profit of **$63,731.44**.
- **The fine print on screen:** a chart of the challenge shows it was **restarted several times in different accounts**:
  - a $2,000 margin account with **6× leverage**;
  - Webull $2,000 with no leverage, then a Webull reset as a cash account;
  - a Robinhood challenge;
  - finally a **Schwab $2,000 margin account with 2× leverage**.

  So "$2K → $65K" is the final run, not a single unbroken one.
- **Position sizes were far bigger than $2,000.** His showcase trade was **5,000 shares at $7.60 = $38,000** [0:40:00]. He also says he was trading his main account during the challenge [0:48:55].

## 2. His 5 steps, in plain words

### Step 1: Pick the right stock ("5 pillars") [0:05:11–0:22:31]

| Pillar | Rule (video and PDFs) |
|---|---|
| Already moving | **Up at least 10%** on the day; his "sweet spot" slide says 30% |
| Unusual volume | **5× its normal volume** or more ("relative volume") |
| A reason | **Breaking news.** He'll skip this if it's the market's #1 gainer |
| Cheap | **$2–$20 a share**; the sweet spot is $5–$10 |
| Few shares available | **Float under 20 million shares**; the PDF says under 10 million |
| Timing | **7–10 AM ET**, ideally a "hot sector" (AI, biotech, crypto, Chinese tech) |

- **His screener cuts the whole market down to 2–4 stocks a day.** The example, YXT, was up more than 500% with a 2.87 million share float, and 44 million shares traded against 500 the day before [0:11:07].
- **He uses his own paid scanner software.** He warns that free scanners run on delayed data [0:09:25].

### Step 2: Wait for the pullback [0:22:42–0:30:00]
After the stock jumps, he waits for a short dip. It's a good dip only if:
1. it gives back **no more than 50%** of the jump;
2. **volume is higher on green candles** than on red ones;
3. it stays **above VWAP**, the day's volume-weighted average price;
4. it stays **above the 9-period EMA**, a short moving average;
5. ideally there are no long "topping tails" (upper wicks).

- **Entry:** "the first candle to make a new high" [0:29:07]. That's the first 1-minute candle to trade above the previous candle's high.
- **Stop:** the low of the pullback [0:29:14]. He aims to make at least 2× what he risks.

### Step 3: Time the entry with Level 2 [0:38:15–0:41:15]
- **Level 2** is the live list of waiting buy and sell orders.
- He leans on round numbers ($7.50, $8, $8.50).
- **On YXT:** bought **5,000 shares at $7.60** with a stop at **$7.45**, risking $750, targeting $8–$8.50.
- He sold in pieces at **$10.10, $9.79 and $9.00**.

### Step 4: Exit signals and when to quit for the day [0:42:55–0:52:22]
- **Get out of a trade when you see any of these:**
  - a big seller on Level 2;
  - a "hidden seller" (lots of buying but the price doesn't move);
  - a burst of red (sell) prints on the tape;
  - a pop then a sharp reversal;
  - buying slowing down;
  - a topping-tail candle or a red candle.
- **Quit for the day if:**
  - you've given back half the day's profit;
  - you've hit your max loss, which he sets equal to his average daily gain;
  - it's past your best time window (10 AM for him);
  - there are no A-quality stocks;
  - the day's momentum is bearish.
- **"Do not come back after I've left."** He took this from Annie Duke's book *Quit*.

### Step 5: Record every trade and study it [0:52:22–0:57:16]
- **Log every trade** in a journal and sort by your biggest losers.
- **His own biggest loser,** INLF, came from chasing at 10:30 AM after he'd already quit for the day, out of FOMO.

## 3. What the public record says about him
**His own trading looks real.**
- An accounting firm, **SingerLewak LLP (March 2026)**, examined his return schedules: **$583 in 2017 to about $18.8M in total gains through 2025**.
- **What it is:** an examination of his return figures, not a full financial audit.
- **What it leaves out:** brokerage fees. It doesn't mention data or software costs.
- **The years are uneven:** 2023 was about $0.31M, 2025 about $6.2M.
- **It's spread across 7+ accounts,** some at offshore brokers.

**His customers are a different story.**
- **In 2022 the FTC sued Warrior Trading** for misleading earnings claims.
  - **Settlement:** **$3 million**, refunded to **20,402 people**. No admission of wrongdoing.
  - **The FTC's main finding:** the company's own simulator data showed **74% of customer accounts lost money, and only 10% made more than $90**.
  - **Course prices:** **$997 + $197/month**, or **$4,297** for 90 days of Pro.
- **His disclaimer, in the video and PDFs:** "My results are not typical. We do not track the typical results of past or current customers."

**The research on this kind of trading:**
- **Retail day traders mostly lose:**
  - **97% lost money** (Brazil, people day trading 300+ days);
  - **fewer than 1% reliably profit** after fees (Taiwan).
- **Chasing "attention" stocks hasn't paid:** stocks that retail traders pile into have underperformed by about **4.7% over the next 20 days** (*Journal of Finance*, 2022).
- **Pump-and-dump risk:** FINRA, the brokerage industry's regulator, warns that **low-float small caps** are frequent targets.
- **No peer-reviewed study tests his exact rules** after costs, trading halts and slippage.

## 5. What it means for you
- **You can't apply it to LITE or Micron.** They're big companies with huge floats, not $2–$20 stocks with under 20 million shares. Ross says himself his system "does not work very well" on large caps [0:05:55].
- **What it would actually take:**
  - real-time scanners and Level 2 (paid data);
  - trading 7–10 AM ET every day;
  - fast decisions on stocks that can drop 20–50% in minutes or get halted.
- **The PDT rule changed:** it was replaced on **June 4, 2026**, so a $25,000 minimum is no longer required. Brokers have until Oct 2027 to switch over, so some may still apply the old limit.
- **Can we test it?** Not properly with what we have.
  - Our data is daily bars for 5,259 stocks, not 1-minute data.
  - We don't have historical float or news data.
  - Under goal part 3 (test only ideas with independent evidence behind them), this one doesn't qualify: the only independent evidence on retail outcomes is negative.
- **What we could do instead:** paper-trade it. Webull and TradingView have free paper accounts, or we could build a paper scanner on Alpaca's free data. Log every trade for 8 weeks, then compare with holding SPY, the same way we're running the MNQ test.
