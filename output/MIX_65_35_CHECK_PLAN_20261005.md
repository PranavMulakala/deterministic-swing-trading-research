# 65% SPY / 35% T-bills: pre-declared comparison check (October 5, 2026)

Written and committed before the numbers were computed. The owner asked after test #10: "test the 65% SPY 35% T-bills mix". I had estimated this mix at about −22% worst drop and ~10% a year, without testing it. This check answers it properly.

**What it is:** not a trading strategy. It's a benchmark mix, to see whether simply holding less SPY gives the same calm as selling puts, with more growth.

## Rules
- **Window:** Jan 4, 2016 – Dec 31, 2025, the same trading days as test #10 (cash-secured puts).
- **SPY:** daily closes adjusted for dividends (`outputs/breakout_data/SPY.csv.gz`), so dividends are reinvested.
- **T-bills:** cash earns the monthly 3-month T-bill rate every calendar day (`research/spy_put_spread_backtest_v1/tbill_3m_monthly.csv`), the same interest assumption as test #10.
- **Main version:** start with $100,000 split 65/35. **Rebalance back to 65/35 at the close on the first trading day of each month.** No trading costs (ETF trades are free at Robinhood) and no taxes.
- **Information only:**
  - **Never rebalanced:** set 65/35 once and leave it.
  - **Rebalanced once a year,** on the first trading day of January.

## Reported (same measures as test #10)
- Final value, per-year growth (CAGR) and worst peak-to-trough drop (on daily values).
- CAGR ÷ |worst drop|.
- Returns in each half (2016–2020 and 2021–2025) and by year.
- Side by side with SPY alone, cash-secured puts (test #10) and T-bills alone.

## The question it answers
Is the mix's worst drop no worse than the puts' −22.2%, while its growth is clearly higher than the puts' 4.46% a year? Nothing will be tuned afterwards: no other splits.
