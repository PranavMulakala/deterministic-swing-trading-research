# Demand and Business-Model Evidence for "Real for Markets" (US retail traders, as of October 18, 2026)

*Scope: demand and economics only. Platform teardowns, crypto/prediction-platform detail, data costs and law are covered by other researchers. All dollar figures are USD. "Est." means a third-party or modeled estimate, not a company disclosure. ## 1. Audience size and activity in 2025–2026 (accounts, active traders, retail share of equity and options volume, 0DTE, crypto, prediction markets)

### Takeaway
The US retail trading audience is large, and in options it is at record activity. Robinhood alone has 28.6M funded customers (August 2026). Options volume hit a record 15.2B contracts in 2025 and is on pace for more than 18B in 2026. 0DTE (same-day expiry) options are about 65–66% of S&P 500 index options volume, with retail a large share of that. Engagement is uneven, though: retail's share of stock trading dipped to about 17% in Q1 2026, crypto app usage is falling, and the fastest-growing retail activity is event contracts (prediction markets).

### Inferences
- The potential audience is tens of millions of US brokerage customers. The highly engaged core is much smaller. Robinhood Gold's 4.8M paying subscribers is a reasonable stand-in for heavy users at the largest retail broker.
- Applying FINRA's 29% social-media-use rate (Section 2) to Robinhood's 28.6M customers gives about 8M+ people at one broker who already use social media for investing. This is rough. Robinhood skews young, so the true share is probably higher.
- Options and especially 0DTE are the most play-by-play-like retail activity: intraday, fast, heavily retail and at record volume. That is the strongest fit for a live per-tick social layer. Overnight and mini-contract records point to markets being "on" for longer each day.
- Growth is in intensity among existing traders, not new entrants. New-investor entry fell from 21% to 8%, and crypto users are falling. A wedge should target current active traders' attention, not onboarding first-time investors.
- Event contracts are the fastest-growing retail activity and the closest thing to sports-style live engagement. Sports are what drive their volume.

### Gaps
- Robinhood does not disclose DAU or MAU. An aggregator lists 13.0M MAU for Q4 2025, which I could not verify against filings. — [Expanded Ramblings](https://expandedramblings.com/index.php/robinhood-statistics-and-facts/)
- No total count of US retail brokerage accounts across all firms was found.
- No primary 2026 source was found for retail share of total US options volume. Cboe-sponsored secondary sources cite 50–60%, which was not verified.
- No primary US user counts were found for Kalshi or Polymarket.

## 2. How retail investors, especially Gen Z and millennials, use social media and online communities for investing; time spent and trusted platforms

### Takeaway
Social media is now a main investing information channel for young US investors. 60% of investors under 35 use it to inform decisions, and 61% of 18–34-year-olds have acted on a finfluencer's (finance influencer's) recommendation, versus 9% and 6% for those 55 and older. YouTube dominates, followed by Reddit, Instagram, Facebook and X. Discord is niche overall but used by roughly a quarter of finfluencer followers (read from a chart). These investors are more active (they check about twice as many sources) and invest partly for entertainment and social reasons. They also score lower on knowledge and lose money to fraud far more often. No reliable data on time spent was found.

### Cited Findings
**FINRA Foundation brief (April 2026; data from the 2024 and 2021 NFCS investor surveys)**
- **Share and overlap**
  - 29% of investors are social media users for investing decisions and 26% follow finfluencers.
  - The groups overlap heavily: 68% of social media users also follow finfluencers, and 77% of followers use social media.
  - The social-media-user share was roughly flat: 30% in 2021, 29% in 2024.
  - [FINRA Foundation brief, Apr 2026](https://finrafoundation.org/sites/finrafoundation/files/2026-03/FINRA_Foundation_Research_Brief_Social_Media_Finfluencers.pdf)
- **By age:** 60% of investors aged 18–34 use social media, versus 9% of those 55+. 61% of 18–34-year-olds made an investment decision based on a social media personality's recommendation, versus 6% of those 55+. — [FINRA Foundation release, Apr 2, 2026](https://www.finra.org/media-center/newsreleases/2026/finra-foundation-research-examines-characteristics-behaviors-outcomes)
- **By experience:** 57% of investors with less than two years of experience use finfluencer recommendations. — [FINRA Foundation, Dec 2025](https://www.finra.org/media-center/newsreleases/2025/new-finra-foundation-research-examines-shifting-investor-behaviors)
- **By portfolio size:** 48% of investors with portfolios under $50K use social media and 47% follow finfluencers. At $500K or more, the figures are 20% and 15%. — [FINRA brief](https://finrafoundation.org/sites/finrafoundation/files/2026-03/FINRA_Foundation_Research_Brief_Social_Media_Finfluencers.pdf)
- **Who they are:** users and followers are mostly younger and male, have smaller portfolios, and are more likely to be people of color. Nearly half don't think of themselves as "typical investors." — [FINRA release, Apr 2026](https://www.finra.org/media-center/newsreleases/2026/finra-foundation-research-examines-characteristics-behaviors-outcomes)
- **Channels, all investors:** YouTube 30%, Reddit 15%, Facebook 14%, LinkedIn 13%, Instagram 13%. YouTube rises to 61% among investors under 35. — [FINRA brief](https://finrafoundation.org/sites/finrafoundation/files/2026-03/FINRA_Foundation_Research_Brief_Social_Media_Finfluencers.pdf); [FINRA Dec 2025](https://www.finra.org/media-center/newsreleases/2025/new-finra-foundation-research-examines-shifting-investor-behaviors)
- **Channels, finfluencer followers:**
  - From the text: YouTube 77%, Instagram 51%, Facebook 49%, Twitter/X 47%, Reddit 46%.
  - Read from the chart, which is hard to parse: TikTok about 40%, LinkedIn about 39%, WhatsApp about 29%, Discord about 25%, Telegram about 20%, Rumble about 12%.
  - [FINRA brief, Figure 1](https://finrafoundation.org/sites/finrafoundation/files/2026-03/FINRA_Foundation_Research_Brief_Social_Media_Finfluencers.pdf)
- **Channels, social media users who don't follow finfluencers:** YouTube 45%, Reddit 28%, Facebook 22%, Instagram 19%, LinkedIn 16%. — [FINRA brief](https://finrafoundation.org/sites/finrafoundation/files/2026-03/FINRA_Foundation_Research_Brief_Social_Media_Finfluencers.pdf)
- **Trend:** 39% used at least one listed social channel in 2021, 41% in 2024. The average number of channels used fell from 1.45 to 1.33. Stocktwits was only asked about in 2021, so there is no current comparable figure for it. — [FINRA brief](https://finrafoundation.org/sites/finrafoundation/files/2026-03/FINRA_Foundation_Research_Brief_Social_Media_Finfluencers.pdf)
- **Information habits, social media users vs non-users:**
  - They consult 7.6 information sources on average, versus 4.0.
  - Mobile apps: 79% vs 19%. Online videos: 86% vs 26%. Podcasts: 73% vs 21%. Financial professionals: 77% vs 69%.
  - [FINRA brief](https://finrafoundation.org/sites/finrafoundation/files/2026-03/FINRA_Foundation_Research_Brief_Social_Media_Finfluencers.pdf)
- **Non-money motives, users vs non-users:** entertainment 59% vs 18%, social activity 59% vs 11%, supporting personal values 66% vs 31%. — [FINRA brief](https://finrafoundation.org/sites/finrafoundation/files/2026-03/FINRA_Foundation_Research_Brief_Social_Media_Finfluencers.pdf)
- **Knowledge vs confidence:** social media users answered 42% of quiz questions correctly, versus 47% for non-users. Yet 63% rated their own knowledge as high, versus 53%. — [FINRA brief](https://finrafoundation.org/sites/finrafoundation/files/2026-03/FINRA_Foundation_Research_Brief_Social_Media_Finfluencers.pdf)

**FINRA Foundation, December 2025 release**
- 13% of investors bought meme stocks or other viral investments, including 29% of those under 35.
- 34% overall, and 62% of those under 35, feel they need to take big risks to reach their financial goals.
- [FINRA Foundation, Dec 2025](https://www.finra.org/media-center/newsreleases/2025/new-finra-foundation-research-examines-shifting-investor-behaviors)

**Other surveys**
- FINRA Foundation and CFA Institute, 2023 Gen Z study: Gen Z learns about investing mainly through social media (48%), internet searches (47%), parents and family (45%) and friends (40%). — [FINRA, May 2023](https://www.finra.org/media-center/newsreleases/2023/finra-foundation-cfa-institute-research-focuses-gen-z-investors)
- CFA Institute, 2024 "Finfluencer Appeal": 37% of US Gen Z investors cite social media influencers as a major factor in deciding to start investing. The figures elsewhere are 30% in Canada, 38% in the UK and 51% in China. — [CFA Institute](https://rpc.cfainstitute.org/sites/default/files/-/media/documents/article/industry-research/finfluencer-report.pdf)
- Gallup (reported May 15, 2025; 2,036 US adults): 42% of 18–29-year-olds use social media for financial information, versus 27% who use financial advisors or planners. Advisor use is 40% at ages 30–49, 45% at 50–64 and 51% at 65+. — [InvestmentNews on Gallup](https://www.investmentnews.com/ria-news/truly-concerning-social-media-is-winning-gen-zs-wallets-over-financial-advisors/260522)

### Inferences
- The behavior already exists at scale but is spread across formats that are not live or not ticker-centric:
  - YouTube: long videos.
  - Reddit and X: text threads.
  - Discord: closed, paid rooms.
  - A real-time per-ticker room would gather existing behavior rather than create new behavior.
- The entertainment and social motives (59% each among users) are the sports-fan parallel that supports the "Real" framing.
- The core audience (young, male, small portfolios, options-active, finfluencer-following) is the most engaged but also brings brokers the least money per person and is the most exposed to fraud. That shapes both how the product can make money and how much trust and safety it needs.
- Social investors already juggle 7.6 sources versus 4.0 for others. That fits the founder's fragmentation thesis, but it is an inference, not a measured complaint.

### Gaps
- **Time spent:** no reliable data on minutes per day spent on investing social content or in trading apps. Robinhood doesn't disclose it, and no Sensor Tower or app-intelligence data turned up.
- **Trust by platform:** no US survey percentages were found. The closest data is not from the US:
  - Australia's ASIC (late 2025, 1,127 people aged 18–28): 56% somewhat or completely trust financial information on social media, and 52% trust finfluencers. — [ASIC](https://www.asic.gov.au/about-asic/news-centre/find-a-media-release/2026-releases/26-049mr-asic-urges-gen-z-to-sense-check-money-advice-as-social-media-fuels-riskier-financial-decisions)
  - CFA Institute's 2022 data found investors under 34 were the most likely to trust social media as a source, but no figure was captured. — [CFA Institute](https://rpc.cfainstitute.org/sites/default/files/-/media/documents/article/industry-research/finfluencer-report.pdf)
- No 2025–2026 Pew data on investing and social media was gathered; the search budget ran out.
- No Gen Z vs millennial split was found in the FINRA data (it uses 18–34, 35–54 and 55+).

## 3. What traders want and hate about current social platforms (noise, scams, no verified track records, fragmentation)

### Takeaway
The best-documented pain is fraud and unverifiable claims. In 2025, $1.1B of the $2.1B lost to scams that began on social media went to investment scams, and investors who rely on social media are far more likely to be targeted and to lose money. The founder's own audits of two paid Discord options-alert groups show the same pattern: only winning screenshots, entry prices followers couldn't get (15–19% worse fills), and losses under any repeatable exit rule. Incumbents see it the same way. Robinhood opened a social feed built on identity-verified trades to all eligible US customers in late September to mid-October 2026.

### Cited Findings
**Scams and fraud**
- FTC, 2025 data (published April 27, 2026):
  - Losses to scams that started on social media reached $2.1B, about 8x 2020's $261M.
  - 28% of people who reported losing money said the scam started on social media.
  - $1.1B, more than half, went to investment scams.
  - Facebook had the most losses, with WhatsApp and Instagram a distant second and third.
  - Common tactics: ads offering to teach investing, WhatsApp groups with fake "successful investor" testimonials, realistic fake platforms that show fake profits and allow small withdrawals, and follow-up "recovery" scams.
  - [FTC Data Spotlight](https://www.ftc.gov/news-events/data-visualizations/data-spotlight/2026/04/reported-losses-scams-social-media-eight-times-higher-2020)
- Press coverage of FTC data: investment scams were the top loss category at $7.9B in 2025, and total reported fraud losses were a record $15.9B, up from $12.5B in 2024. — [TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2026/04/27/consumers-lost-2-1-billion-to-social-media-scams-in-2025-ftc-reports/); [BleepingComputer](https://www.bleepingcomputer.com/news/security/ftc-americans-lost-over-21-billion-to-social-media-scams-in-2025/) (secondary; not checked against FTC's own release)
- FINRA, social media users vs non-users:
  - Targeted for fraud: 13% vs 3%.
  - Lost money when targeted: 68% vs 29%.
  - Could not spot fraud red flags: 69% vs 42%.
  - Finfluencer followers vs non-followers on the same measures: 16% vs 3%, 69% vs 26%, 72% vs 42%.
  - The losses and the red-flag gap stay significant after controlling for knowledge and age.
  - [FINRA brief](https://finrafoundation.org/sites/finrafoundation/files/2026-03/FINRA_Foundation_Research_Brief_Social_Media_Finfluencers.pdf)
- Investors who lost money to fraud most often used YouTube (79%), TikTok (70%) and Instagram (67%). — [FINRA brief](https://finrafoundation.org/sites/finrafoundation/files/2026-03/FINRA_Foundation_Research_Brief_Social_Media_Finfluencers.pdf)
- 37% of investors worry about losing money to fraud, up from 31% in 2021. — [FINRA Dec 2025](https://www.finra.org/media-center/newsreleases/2025/new-finra-foundation-research-examines-shifting-investor-behaviors)
- Social media users do more checking, not less: 36% have looked up a financial professional's background with a regulator, versus 14% of non-users. — [FINRA release, Apr 2026](https://www.finra.org/media-center/newsreleases/2026/finra-foundation-research-examines-characteristics-behaviors-outcomes)

**The founder's own audits of paid alert groups (October 2026 files in this repo)**
- **EdwardAlerts VIP**
  - The founder paid about $60. The audit tested 26 VIP option alerts from September 9 to October 9, 2026 against OPRA minute bars, at $300 per alert.
  - Results by exit rule: his stated trim rule −$1,215 (7 of 26 winners); selling all at his first post −$981; holding everything −$1,919; the founder's own +50% limit −$1,844.
  - Only hindsight exits made money (+$3,593).
  - Losers were mostly never followed up in the alerts channel.
  - Followers paid on average 15% above his posted price. One AAPL 0DTE alert was posted at $1.00 while trading at $1.62.
  - [EDWARD_VIP_REVIEW_20261012.md](/Users/pranavmulakala/personal/deterministic-swing-trading-research/output/EDWARD_VIP_REVIEW_20261012.md)
- **"Demon Alerts" (Optionality Discord)**
  - 30 buy alerts from September 18 to October 9, 2026.
  - All 35 screenshots he posted were cropped green %-gain strips, with no dollars, contract count, fill or exit. None covered any of the 18 losing alerts.
  - A follower buying in the next minute paid on average 19% more than the posted price, and up to 50% more.
  - The alerts are sold as one of about 15 channels in a paid "Optionality Pro" tier, with a referral program and warnings about impersonators asking for money.

**What incumbents and analogs are building or abandoning**
- Robinhood Social:
  - First pitched in September 2025 as a rival to Reddit's trading communities. Beta began in March 2026 with about 1,000 invited HOOD Summit attendees.
  - Robinhood's X account said on September 29, 2026 that Social had reached all customers. CEO Vlad Tenev confirmed the wider rollout on October 13, 2026.
  - Profiles are identity-verified (KYC). Users post verified trades and can share charts, realized profit and loss, and portfolio allocations. They can follow traders and get news and company updates in the feed.
  - There is no copy trading; copying is manual. Futures and multi-leg options can't be shared yet. Licensed professionals are barred. The desktop platform, Legend, gets Social "in the coming weeks."
  - No engagement metrics have been disclosed.
  - [Coinspectator, Oct 14, 2026](https://coinspectator.com/mainstream/2026/10/14/robinhood-lets-all-eligible-us-customers-share-their-trades-and-portfolios/); [Robinhood newsroom, beta](https://robinhood.com/newsroom/robinhood-social-beta/); [Android Headlines](https://www.androidheadlines.com/2026/03/robinhood-verified-social-trading-network-beta.html)

- Real, the sports-app analog:
  - A co-founder describes sports content on social media as "fragmented and sensationalized."

### Inferences
- Verified, timestamped track records with realistic fills are the most defensible "trust" feature. Robinhood now offers verified trades to its own customers, so an independent app would have to differ by working across brokers, by attaching a timestamp and the live price to every call, or by computing what a follower would actually have earned.
- The alert-group audits suggest a concrete feature. Automatically stamp the live bid and ask when any call is posted, then show follower P&L. That would make the 15–19% entry gap and the cherry-picked screenshots visible, which could attract honest alert sellers and their buyers.
- Public.com's exit is a caution: a broker's social feed of recaps can be replaced by AI summaries. Live reactions to price moves (the Real model) are a different behavior from news recaps, but that hasn't been proven in markets.
- Trust and safety (scam links, impersonators, pump-and-dump coordination) are core product costs, since the target users are the most fraud-exposed group.

### Gaps
- No survey data was found quantifying complaints about noise, bots, fragmentation or missing track records on Stocktwits, X, Reddit or Discord.
- Recurring Reddit and X discussion themes were not mined. The shared search budget ran out before this step.
- No independent engagement numbers were found for Robinhood Social or Stocktwits.

## 6. Social and live-event app benchmarks: D1 and D30 retention, and what drives retention in live-event apps

### Takeaway
Finance apps typically keep about 17–33% of new users on Day 1 (D1) and about 8–11% at Day 30 (D30); entertainment apps keep about 4–9% at D30. The live-sports analog Real shows what live moments can do: 700–900K daily active users on 1.3M monthly active users (a DAU/MAU of about 54–69%), about 50M push notifications a day, and about 220K unique users on a single big game night. The evidence on what drives retention (well-timed pushes on key moments, live polls and predictions, identity and karma) comes mostly from vendors.

### Cited Findings
- AppsFlyer benchmarks (undated; the linked source is a 2021 report):
  - Finance: D1 16.96% on Android and 21.58% on iOS; D30 9.27% and 10.74%.
  - Entertainment: D1 17.10% and 17.83%; D30 4.65% and 4.42%.
  - Casino and sports betting: D1 28.15% and 25.05%; D30 12.47% and 7.56%.
  - [AppsFlyer glossary](https://www.appsflyer.com/glossary/retention-rate/)
- A secondary table citing AppsFlyer 2025 and Adjust 2025 benchmarks gives D1 / D7 / D30 of:
  - Finance: 26–27% / 18–19% / 8%.
  - Entertainment: 28% / 18% / 8–9%.
  - Games: 27–32% / 13–18% / 5–8%.
  - [Engagelab](https://www.engagelab.com/blog/increase-app-retention)
- OneSignal 2024 benchmarks, via GetStream, D1 / D7 / D30:
  - Finance: 26.84% / 18.54% / 8.03%.
  - News: 26.67% / 15.83% / 7.55%.
  - Cross-category average: 28.29% / 17.86% / 7.88%.
  - [GetStream](https://getstream.io/blog/app-retention-guide/)
- Fintech: 33% / 16% / 9.2% (D1 / D7 / D30), attributed to Adjust, AppsFlyer and Sensor Tower (secondary). — [Digital Applied](https://www.digitalapplied.com/blog/mobile-app-marketing-statistics-2026-install-data)
- Users who got at least one push notification in their first 90 days were 3x more likely to stay (Airship, 2023). — [GetStream](https://getstream.io/blog/app-retention-guide/)
- **Real (sports), as of April 2026:**
  - 700K–900K daily active users and 1.3M monthly active users.
  - About 50M push notifications a day.
  - About 220K unique users engaged during Bam Adebayo's 83-point game in March 2026.
  - 18 leagues covered.
  - Earlier milestones: 1,000 unique users on an NBA game in 2021, and server crashes during the January 2022 Chiefs–Bills playoff game.
  - Funding: a Chernin Group-led minority round (amount undisclosed), plus Pear Ventures and NBA players.
  - [Front Office Sports, Apr 13, 2026](https://frontofficesports.com/chernin-group-funding-round-real-app/)
- An earlier (circa 2023) Real profile reported about 170K monthly active users and more than 3M comments in 30 days. It also described per-play reaction counters and threads, karma leaderboards and no real-money betting. — [BetCrunch](https://www.betcrunch.com/p/a-real-sports-app-thats-built-on). Total funding is listed at about $12.1M; round history is unclear across sources. — [VCBacked](https://www.vcbacked.co/company/real-sports-app)
- Live-event engagement drivers (vendor sources):
  - IBM's 2025 Sports Fan Survey: 82% of in-person attendees use apps during events, and multi-device following of sports rose from 27% to 29%.
  - Notifications tied to live moments outperform generic promotions.
  - TNT Sports saw push open rates rise 28% and live viewing sessions run 25% longer after pairing pushes with in-app messages.
  - [Choicely](https://www.choicely.com/blog/how-sports-fans-consume-content-and-where-your-app-fits-in)
  - Polls sent during or right after a match get far higher participation. A claim that fans who complete a quiz each game have 2.3x higher 30-day retention is unverified. — [SportsFirst](https://www.sportsfirst.net/post/how-to-boost-user-engagement-in-your-sports-app)

### Inferences
- Markets have a daily "game" (a 6.5-hour session on about 252 days a year), scheduled spikes (earnings, CPI, Fed meetings, 0DTE expiries) and 24/7 crypto and overnight sessions. That is more frequent than any sports league's schedule, which could support higher usage frequency than Real. The trade-off is less scarcity, so each "game" may feel less like an event.
- Real's mechanics map onto markets:
  - Fast data first: live price, chart and the news headline.
  - A push at key moments: a price move, an earnings print, a halt.
  - One-tap reactions on each move.
  - Identity and karma.
  - Predictions without real money, or verified trade cards.
- Real's DAU/MAU of about 54–69% far exceeds what finance-app D30 retention of about 8–11% implies. These are different metrics, but the gap shows live, moment-driven products can build habits well above category norms.
- Real has been live since at least 2021 and reached 1.3M monthly users by April 2026, in sports, a far larger fan base. A markets version should plan for a smaller audience worth more per user, and slower organic growth.

### Gaps
- No primary 2025–2026 AppsFlyer or Adjust retention figures for social or sports app categories; no Sensor Tower data was found.
- No retention or DAU/MAU data for Stocktwits, Robinhood Social, Discord trading servers or Real's market-hours equivalents.
- Real's retention curve (D1/D30) and revenue are not disclosed.

### Inferences
- Tailwinds for live social around stocks and options:
  - Record options and 0DTE activity.
  - The PDT repeal letting small accounts day trade.
  - Stocks near record highs.
  - Prediction markets making "a market on every event" normal.
- Headwinds:
  - Crypto is cooling, so a crypto-first wedge is weaker right now.
  - Retail net buying is slowing and new investors aren't arriving, so growth has to come from capturing the attention of existing active traders.
  - A real correction could cut engagement. The 2021–2022 history wasn't researched here.
- The window for "verified trades" as the only wedge is narrowing now that Robinhood ships it to its own customers. The wedge needs one or more of:
  - Neutrality across brokers.
  - A clearly faster, cleaner Real-style live interface.
  - A sharp niche with built-in "games," such as live 0DTE/SPX sessions or earnings-night threads.

- Gallup's 2026 stock-ownership figure was not verified. A search-result summary attributed 58% in April 2026 (down from 62%) to secondary reports, but Gallup's own page still shows 2025 data (62%).

- The bull-market read is based on SPY monthly data only. Breadth, volatility (VIX) and earnings-season context weren't analyzed.
